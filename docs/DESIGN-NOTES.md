# Design notes — the reporting figures

Why the three figures look the way they do. All of them are generated by
[`charts/generate_charts.py`](../charts/generate_charts.py); nothing is hand-drawn, so a change in
the data changes the figures.

## Form follows the question

The outbound dataset is tiny — two graded calls — so the trap there is reaching for a dashboard
that implies more signal than exists. The inbound dataset has fourteen, which is enough to ask a
question but not enough for distribution machinery. Each panel answers one question, and where the
honest answer was "this is a number, not a chart", a stat tile or a table row is used instead.

| Panel | Question | Form | Why not something else |
|---|---|---|---|
| KPI row | What are the headline numbers? | Stat tiles | A five-bar bar chart of five unrelated measures says nothing |
| Who did the talking | Who dominated the conversation? | Horizontal stacked bar, one row per call | Part-to-whole, two series, long category names — horizontal reads better than vertical |
| Judge verdict | What did the judge decide, and why? | Status badge + the judge's own sentence | The verdict is categorical and the *reason* is the useful part; a chart would discard it |
| Campaign funnel | Where did the leads go? | Ordinal single-hue bars | Ordered stages, so one hue stepped light→dark, not five categorical colours |
| Cost & duration | What did each call cost? | Table | Two measures with different units — putting them on one plot would need two axes, which invents a correlation that is not in the data |
| Conversation flow | What actually happened, turn by turn? | Paired swimlanes, one block per turn, sized by words | Shows turn-taking *and* imbalance in one read; a bar chart of turn counts loses the alternation |
| Grade mix | What did the judge decide, across 14 calls? | Three status bars with counts | A pie would hide the fact that the middle bucket holds two thirds of the calls |
| Talk share by grade | Does the agent talking more go with a worse grade? | One dot per call, grouped by grade, with a group median tick | **No.** With n≤3 per group a bar of means would draw three confident bars out of nine dots; the dots show the overlap, which is the answer |
| How calls ended | Did the caller hang up, or did the system give up? | One stacked bar, two steps of a single hue, plus a legend | The two segments are not identities, so they do not get two categorical hues; splitting a bar that is two thirds one value also needs no second row |
| Spend against call length | Does price follow duration? | Scatter, one x and one y, against the pooled rate | Two measures of different units *can* share a plot when one is genuinely a function of the other — but never on two y-scales |

## Showing a metric that does not work

The most useful panel here is the one where the expected story fails. Talk share is the intuitive
quality signal, and on the outbound two-call sample it moved with the grade; at fourteen calls it
does not (the Bad calls are the most agent-dominated group). The panel therefore plots every call
rather than the group means, marks each group's median, and says in its subtitle what a
relationship would have looked like if there were one. A chart that only gets drawn once the
number is flattering is not instrumentation.

The spend panel does the same job in the other direction: cost *is* almost entirely a function of
call length (r = 0.99), so the pooled rate is drawn as a reference line — and the panel then labels
the two calls that break it, because the exception is where the money is.

## Colour

Colour was assigned last, by the job it does:

- **Categorical — identity.** The two speakers are the only two genuine series, so they take fixed
  categorical slots 1 and 2 (blue `#2a78d6`, orange `#eb6834`). Slots are assigned to the *entity*
  (agent, caller) and stay put — filtering or reordering never repaints them.
- **Status — state.** The judge's verdict is a state, not an identity, so it uses the reserved
  status palette (good `#0ca30c`, warning `#fab219`, critical `#d03b3b`) rather than a categorical
  slot — on the grade bars, the talk-share dots and the spend dots alike. A status colour never
  carries meaning alone: every use pairs it with the word "Good" / "Neutral" / "Bad", and the spend
  panel carries a legend as well.
- **Ordinal — magnitude across ordered stages.** The funnel uses one hue in three steps
  (`#86b6ef` → `#3987e5` → `#1c5cab`), light to dark. A value ramp on nominally-ordered
  categories would be wrong; a funnel is genuinely ordered, so the ramp is correct. The two steps
  reused in the "How calls ended" bar are not claiming an order — they separate two parts of one
  whole, so the hue carries no meaning the labels do not.
- **No colour for the reference line.** The pooled rate is drawn in muted ink, not a series hue,
  because it is not data — it is the axis of the argument.

### Validated, not eyeballed

Both palettes were checked with the colorblind-safety validator rather than judged by eye
(OKLab ΔE ×100, 8 = target, 15 = normal-vision floor):

```
categorical  #2a78d6,#eb6834   light  → ALL CHECKS PASS
  worst adjacent CVD ΔE 24.7 (protan), normal-vision ΔE 33.6, both ≥ 3:1 on surface

ordinal      #86b6ef,#3987e5,#1c5cab  light  → ALL CHECKS PASS
  monotone lightness, adjacent ΔL ≥ 0.06, light end 2.06:1 on surface, hue spread 3°
```

A three-slot categorical palette would also clear the all-pairs gate, but two series is all this
data has. No eighth-and-beyond hue is ever generated.

## Marks

- Bars are capped at 22px with a 4px rounded data-end; the baseline end stays square.
- **Turns are separated by a 2px gap in the surface colour**, not by a stroke. Drawing a border
  around a mark to separate it adds ink that is not data.
- Defect badges carry a 2px surface ring so they read cleanly over the lane they sit above.
- Gridlines and rules are solid 1px hairlines one step off the surface — never dashed. Dashing
  reads as "projection" or "threshold" when it is only a grid.
- Text always wears an ink token, never a series colour. The one exception is a label set *inside*
  a filled segment, which picks white or ink by the fill's luminance.

## A static figure has no tooltip

These are committed SVG files rendered in a README, so there is no hover layer and nothing may
depend on one. Every value that matters is therefore read directly: talk-share percentages sit on
their segments, the funnel labels its own bars, cost and duration are a table, the talk-share
strip labels each group's median and tags the three calls under 30 seconds, and the scatter names
the two calls whose price breaks the rate. The figures would survive being printed.

## Self-checking layout

`generate_charts.py` estimates the rendered right edge of every text element it draws and
`bounds_check()` fails loudly if any of them runs past the card's inner margin. On the inbound
figure it caught an annotation that crossed the reference line — a point label drawn inside the
plot cannot know where the line runs, so it became a caption under the axis instead. Earlier it
caught two more: turn blocks overflowing the card because the 2px inter-turn gaps were not
reserved in the shared word→pixel scale, and a rubric subtitle running 100px past the edge. Layout
bugs in generated SVG are invisible in the data and obvious only in a render, so the generator
proves it fits instead of hoping.
